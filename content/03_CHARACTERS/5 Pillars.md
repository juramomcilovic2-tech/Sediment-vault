The five pillars of Otti is a story and character framework for the game. Since this is a smaller scale project, one of the biggest possible hurdles and complications is rapid expansion of ideas and scope that can easily become fluff, unnecessary or drastically prolong development. 
To combat this, ***5 Pillars*** is a framework that limits the scope, but doesn't choke out the project for potential ideas. 

Regarding areas/zones, this was used to limit area count, instead focusing on 5 main areas that will then be developed in-depth and give them the *sauce*. 

Regarding characters, this framework should apply to the main roster. In this case of worldbuilding and narrative, similar problems arise. I expect there will be many ideas for possible story arcs and character developments, and this rule is supposed to help with properly treating those ideas so that the character count doesn't sky-rocket. 

The main roster should consist of 5 characters that will be the primary focus and lens through which the player takes in the game world. These have to be written well and the player has to spend more time with them. Other optional and side-characters will not be limited to five. 


### MAIN ROSTER:
Otti City is a place of technological advancements, but also of social dissonance, a post-industrial city that is emotionally depleted. To reflect this, the main cast should be strategically placed to represent the *loose threads* of a society struggling to find a new pattern.

1. The Factory: Shindo
Shindo is a model of automaton that is being produced in the factory. At first, he seems like just another security bot, but as you interact with him more and more, you start feeling his *metal anxieties*. There are rumours circulating that he and his band of automatons will soon be replaced by the new Torus 1 models.  
**The Problem:** **Obsolescence**: Shindo is a mass-produced security model. His biggest fear currently is that they will close-out production on his model, which means no more replacement parts, and no more Shindo. His fear is that if he no longer serves his intended function, then what right does he have to exist? It doesn't help that residents are scared of him, calling him automaton slurs like:
*wire-back, clanker* and *switch-hitter*.

**The Goal:** Through Shindo, the player explores the need for uncompromising efficiency. The realization that efficiency is a cold comfort when you are no longer efficient.

2. The Archive: Qu-Bit

**The Problem: Binary** In a post-industrial city obsessed with optimization, Qu-bit represents the cold pressure to be "fixed", to be either a **1 (useful)** or a **0 (waste)**. She is the gatekeeper who views human complexity as data noise that needs to be filed away. 

Qu-bit begins as a rigid, binary computer, one of the archive's main units for complex filing and algorithms. She performs the role the Archive demands, carrying the weight of her family’s (older computer models and looms) like a restrictive uniform. Her arc is a hopeful journey of self-actualization as she *goes quantum* in the story.

Through the player’s encouragement, Qu-bit stops calculating and starts witnessing. She accelerates into a **Quantum State** where she transcends her original computer binary.
