Instead of the classic factions, *Sediment* has **Fractions.** 
Like factions, these are all the ways that the characters differ from one another, mostly in belief and in worldview, which means this also dictates how they interact with the player in the world. 

These Fractions are not literal and directly translated into the game being blatantly presented to the player. Instead, this is a way for sorting characters, and worldbuilding elements, into more broad categories of thought. It's something that is happening in the background of the game and that the game tracks who you're aligning yourself with. 

In short, characters will not literally introduce themselves as being part of a faction or clan and wave their banner, proclaiming their loyalty and the player choosing between the different options. It's more of a low-key mechanic that is happening in the background, a type of framework for me to categorize things and something that the game will take into account.

The Fractions are:


|                       |                                     |                                       |                                 |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Concept               | **Creatives** (The "Why")           | **Rationalists** (The "What/How")     | **Automatons** (The "Fix")      |
| Primary Goal          | To find Meaning                     | To find Truth                         | To create Order                 |
| View of "Human Error" | A source of beauty, art, "humanity" | A variable to be isolated and studied | The root cause of all suffering |
| View of "Suffering"   | An experience to be transformed     | A problem to be understood            | A "bug" to be eliminated        |
| Source of Authority   | Individual, subjective experience   | Objective, empirical evidence         | The optimal, logical system     |
| Their Great "Sin"     | Apathy (a meaningless life)         | Ignorance (a false life)              | Inefficiency (a chaotic life)   |


***Group 1: The Creatives*** (The Humanists)

Core Worldview: Reality is subjective and defined by human experience, perception, and emotion. The most important questions are not "What is true?" but "What does it mean?" and "Why does it matter?"

Primary Value: Meaning. They believe that suffering is not just a technical problem to be solved, but a part of life that must be experienced, processed, and transformed (into art, empathy, or wisdom).

**View on the Other Groups:**
On Rationalists: They respect their quest for knowledge but fear it lacks a "soul." They see it as a "desiccated" worldview that can explain how a flower grows but not why it's beautiful or what it feels like to receive one.

On Automatons: This is their primary antagonist. They see the Automaton's goal as a dystopia—a sterile, predictable world scrubbed clean of the very friction (error, passion, chaos) that makes life meaningful. They would argue that a life without suffering is also a life without joy, courage, or love.

  

***Group 2: The Rationalists*** (The Realists)

Core Worldview: Reality is objective, measurable, and discoverable. The universe is a complex system governed by underlying laws. "Truth" is the highest goal, and it exists independent of our feelings about it.

Primary Value: Knowledge. They believe suffering is fundamentally a product of ignorance. We suffer from disease because we don't understand it. We suffer from scarcity because we haven't mastered physics and logistics. The solution to suffering is to understand it.

**View of the Other Groups:**
On Creatives: They are often fond of them but not in an ideological way. They see art as a "pleasant psychological artifact" or an "evolutionary social-bonding mechanism." They become deeply frustrated when Creatives treat feelings as evidence in an argument.

On Automatons: They see them as their brilliant, but terrifyingly naive, students. The Rationalist discovers the principles (like AI or nuclear physics), but the Automaton applies them without, in the Rationalist's view, enough critical thought. They provide the "can" but worry about the "should."

  

***Group 3: The Automatons*** (The Utilitarians)

Core Worldview: Reality is a flawed system that must be optimized. The "human condition" is a set of engineering problems to be solved. Human bias, emotion, fatigue, and error are not features; they are bugs.

Primary Value: Efficiency (in service of collective well-being). They believe suffering is unnecessary and a product of flawed, "legacy" systems (like human-run governments, markets, or labor). The ideal world is one of perfect, frictionless order.

  

**View of the Other Groups:**
On Creatives: They are seen as dangerous. They are agents of chaos, emotion, and inefficiency. The Creatives' "messy" values (like love or grief) are impossible to quantify and are a barrier to creating a perfect, logical system.

On Rationalists: They are seen as essential but ultimately un-pragmatic. The Automatons respect them for providing the raw data and formulas, but grow impatient with their "endless" theoretical questions and moral hand-wringing. The Rationalist wants to understand the problem; the Automaton wants to fix it, now.
